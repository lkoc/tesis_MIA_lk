Thermal performance optimization of underground power cable system is presented in this paper. The
analyzed system consists of three underground power cables situated in an in-line arrangement. The
HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene) casing pipes, ﬁlled with SBM (Sand-Bentonite Mixture), covers the
cables to protect them from heavy mechanical loads (e.g. vibrations). The FTB (Fluidized Thermal Backﬁll)
layer is applied to prevent the cables from overheating. Due to the substantial costs of FTB backﬁll
material (in relation to the native soil or dry sand), the cross-sectional area of FTB bedding layer has to be
minimized. Furthermore, the maximum cable conductor temperature is expected not to exceed the
optimum operating temperature. Therefore, the optimization procedure i.e. momentum-type PSO (Par-
ticle Swarm Optimization) is applied. The FEM (Finite Element Method) is used to solve the two-
dimensional steady-state heat conduction problem. As a result, temperature distribution is deter-
mined for the native soil, FTB bedding, and cables. The performed computations considered the tem-
perature dependent current rating and volumetric heat generation rate from cable conductor. The
applied optimization procedure resulted in determination of the optimum cable spacing and cross-
sectional area of the rectangular-shaped FTB bedding layer. Moreover, the obtained maximum temper-
ature for the cable core do not exceed the allowable value.

1. Introduction
In recent years, the thermal analysis of underground power
cable system attracted a broad scientiﬁc attention. In most in-
stances, the electric energy transmission line operates at the
maximum possible conductor electric current. According to ﬁrst
Joule's law, heat generated in power cable core depends on its
electric resistance and ﬂowing electric current. An accurate analysis
of heat dissipation process from the underground power cables to
the surrounding soil plays a crucial role in designing the electricity
transmission lines in modern power plants [1,2].
Ampacity is deﬁned as the maximum electrical current that a
conductor can safely carry without exceeding its insulation tem-
perature limitations. Power cable ampacity is also described as a
current carrying capacity. The current carrying capacity mostly
depends on the temperature of the cable conductor. Excessive
conductor
temperature
leads
to
cable
overheating
and
the
improper operation of the power transmission line. Furthermore,
the better conditions of heat dissipation process from a cable to
its surroundings, the lower cable diameter may be used for
the same electrical load. Therefore, the unit costs associated
with
underground
transmission
line
installation
decreases
signiﬁcantly.
The traditional method used in calculations of the thermal
resistance between the cable system and the external environment
[3,4] assumes that the soil is a homogeneous material with con-
stant thermal conductivity. In fact, the heat transfer processes
associated with heat dissipation from the power cable to the sur-
rounding soil are more complex. The soil is a multilayered porous
material and consists of e.g. quartz, organic matter, clay minerals,
air, water in the liquid and vapor phase, among others. Moreover,
the heat transfer conditions depend strongly on the thermal con-
ductivity of each soil layer. Thus, soil thermal conductivity exhibits
a distinct dependence on porosity, liquid vapor transport, and
temperature, among others [5]. The thermal conductivity increases
with soil porosity since the pores are ﬁlled with water and the air

\n[6,7]. The higher soil water content, the better heat dissipation
process conditions occur. However, water existing in pores may
locally evaporate in the immediate vicinity of the cable thus the so-
called ‘dry zones’ are created. Dry zones are usually formed around
underground power cables under loading conditions due to the
moisture migration within the soil [8]. The presence of dry zones
around the cable results in a signiﬁcant soil thermal conductivity
drop since the thermal conductivity is over 20 times lower for a
vapor than for liquid. Therefore, when temperature is rising, and
dry zones are formed, it leads to a signiﬁcant reduction in soil
thermal conductivity.
Geometrical conﬁguration of the cable system (i.e. cable
spacing, shape of the trench, backﬁll layer cross-sectional area)
inﬂuences the cable core temperature. In thermal engineering
practice, it is possible to enhance the heat transfer from the solid
materials to its surroundings by different methods. In particular
cases: extended surfaces [9e11], porous structures [12e14] and
multiphase materials [15,16] are utilized. During the underground
transmission line placement, the heat dissipation from power ca-
bles to the surrounding soil shall be enhanced.
In order to circumvent the power cable overheating problems the
specially designed bedding material, e.g. FTB (Fluidized Thermal
Backﬁll), exhibiting higher thermal conductivity than the mother
ground, is often used. Moreover, the thermal conductivity of the dry
FTB material is over three times greater than for the dry soil.
Therefore, underground power cables placement in the FTB bedding
instead of the mother ground may circumvent the problems asso-
ciated with a considerable thermal conductivity drop in soil, espe-
cially in the vicinity of the cable. On the other hand, the material
costs related to FTB backﬁlling are signiﬁcantly higher, comparing to
the mother ground. Approximate cost of FTB is about $130 per cubic
meter [17] and strongly depends on the cable line installation
location and availability of raw materials, among others. Hence,
when designing the shape of FTB layer (the optimum cross-sectional
area of the bedding layer), the optimization work has to be done.
This paper presents an optimization of the FTB bedding layer
cross-sectional area for underground power cable system that is
planned to be installed in one of the Polish power plants. The
analyzed system consists of three underground power cables sit-
uated in an in-line arrangement. Minimization of the FTB bedding
cross-sectional area is performed by employing the momentum-
type PSO (Particle Swarm Optimization) Method [18]. Moreover,
the cable conductor temperature cannot exceed the optimum
operating temperature i.e. 65 C, which is speciﬁed by the cable
producer. The Finite Element Method [5,19,20,23,24] is applied to
determine the temperature distribution in underground power
cable system. The methods for thermal analysis of underground
power cable systems can be also found in Refs. [21,22].

\n2. Solutions for installation of underground cable systems
Direct burial power cable laying technique is widely used in
most countries due to the low operational costs and immunity to
adverse weather conditions. Application of light mortar or thermal
ﬁller instead of ﬁne sand may considerably improve the circuit
transmission properties. The power cable transmission line may be
laid in the ground in a different conﬁgurations [25]. Typical for-
mations include trefoil (triangular) and ﬂat (in-line) arrangement
(Fig. 1). The appropriate type of cables arrangement depends on
several factors like screen bonding method, conductor area and
available space for installation.
FTB is one of the commonly used and popular backﬁll materials.
It is the engineered slurry backﬁll mixture which, when solidiﬁed,
may develop into an efﬁcient heat conducting medium [26].
The underground power cables are commonly situated in ducts.
This laying method has a signiﬁcant advantage over the conven-
tional burial technique. The power cables are usually located in the
PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride) or PE (Polyethylene) casing pipes. Cable
placement in ducts is widely applied when the ground is subjected
to unusually heavy loads (i.e. signiﬁcant vibrations) e.g. under
crossings level (Fig. 1). Flat formation is applied to particular cases
i.e. protected cables: 225 and 400 kV auxiliary, under road crossings
[27]. The PVC or PE casing pipes are ﬁlled with Sand-Bentonite
Mixture (SBM). SBM improves the heat dissipation from hot cable
core to the surrounding soil by increasing the thermal conductivity
in the vicinity of the cable line [28].
Bentonite, which is commercially available in powder form, is a
swelling montmorillonite clay which, when mixed with water,
expands and forms a gel. In gel form, it acts as a lubricant or
ﬂuidizer for the mixture [29]. Thermal conductivity of SMB mainly
depends on three parameters i.e. mixture constituents (sand/
bentonite) proportions, dry density, and water content [30]. An
increase in the SBM water content and dry density improves
thermal conductivity of the mixture [31]. Also, the proper propor-
tion of individual components inﬂuence the SBM thermal proper-
ties. Due to the swelling of bentonite particles the better contact
between the grains is achieved.
This paper presents the thermal analysis of 400 kV high voltage
underground power cables [32] placed in the ﬂat formation. Table 1
gives all the power cable relevant parameters. According to the
producer requirements [33] the maximum allowable cable core
temperature shall not be exceeded. When the cable core temper-
ature is greater than 90 C, the XLPE cable insulation melting may
occur leading directly to the transmission line malfunction. The
optimum power cable operating temperature is speciﬁed as 65 C.
Fig. 2 depicts the structure of considered high voltage power
cable with a conductor diameter of 49.6 mm and cross-sectional
area of 1600 mm2.
Table 2 lists the materials, thermal conductivities and thick-
nesses of the individual cable components.

\n3.1. Momentum type Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

dynamic penalty function with a continues assignment to solve a
discrete nonlinear constrained global optimization problem. As it

faster convergence than the traditional PSO algorithm [34e36]. The

4. Determination of the temperature distribution in
underground power cable system
The FEM (Finite Element Method) is used to determine the
temperature distribution in the thermal system presented in Fig. 3.
The analyzed power cable system consists of three 400 kV single
core underground power cables situated in the HDPE casing pipes,
ﬁlled with SBM. The ﬂat formation is considered. The casing pipes
are placed in FTB layer with rectangular-shaped cross-section, in
order to prevent the power cables from overheating. It is assumed
that the FTB layer is buried in the mother ground with constant
thermal properties. As above-mentioned, since the geometrical
symmetry exists thus, only a half of the real thermal system (Fig. 1)
is considered.
4.1. Material properties
The mother ground thermal conductivity ksoil is assumed to be
constant and equal to 1.00 W/(m K), according to IEC standards [3].
In this case application of SGFC mixture (Sand, Gravel, Fly ash and
Cement-mix), is proposed. SGFC is one of the FTB types with a given
composition: 41% of ﬁne aggregate, 49% of coarse aggregate, 2.5%
of cement and 7.5% of ﬂy ash (ﬂuidizer). When dry conditions
occur, SGFC mixture is characterized by dry density of 2187 kg/m3
and thermal conductivity of 1.54 W/(m K) [29].
The power cables are situated in HDPE casing pipes ﬁlled
with sand-bentonite-based buffer material with a dry density of
1700 kg/m3 and thermal conductivity of 0.95 W/(m K) [37]. The
external diameter and thickness of HDPE casing pipe are 278 mm
and 14 mm, respectively. Mixture proposed by Ref. [37] is
composed of MX-80 (Na-bentonite), sand and water. Sand mass
fraction and water contents are 37.5% and 8.17%, respectively [37].
Table 4 shows the thermal conductivity of different materials
considered in this study.
The computational study considers the dry conditions of FTB
and SBM backﬁlls, what ensures the safety margin when the power
cable core temperature is calculated. In real conditions, the heat
dissipation process is more efﬁcient, due to the moisture existing in
the soil and backﬁll pores [38,39].

6. Summary
This study presents the application of momentum-type Particle
Swarm Optimization (PSO) for the thermal performance optimi-
zation of underground power cable system. An electrical energy
transmission line that consists of three underground power cables,
placed in a ﬂat (in-line) arrangement, is considered. To avoid the
excessive mechanical loads the cables are laid in HDPE casing pipes,
ﬁlled with SBM. The HDPE casing pipes are buried directly in Flu-
idized Thermal Backﬁll (FTB) layer to protect the cables from
overheating. Due to the substantial costs of FTB backﬁll material
(about $130 per cubic meter), the cross-sectional area of FTB
bedding layer need to be minimized. The momentum-type Particle
Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm is used for this purpose.
The geometrical dimensions of FTB bedding layer and the cable
spacing are used as design variables in the optimization procedure.
The particle swarms that consists of Npar ¼ 10 and Npar ¼ 30 par-
ticles, containing the values of design variables, are considered in
the computations. The behavior of the following design variables is
studied during the algorithm operation (Fig. 4b):
 cable spacing l,
 distance from the cable to the top edge of FTB bedding layer b,
 distance from the cable to the bottom edge of FTB bedding layer
p,
 distance from the cable to the right edge of FTB bedding layer s.
The results comparison for the different swarm sizes allowed to
check its inﬂuence on the obtained optimum solution. The iteration
number considered in the PSO optimization is 200. The tempera-
ture distribution in the cable system is obtained by means of the
Finite Element Method under the assumption that resulting tem-
perature ﬁeld is steady-state and two-dimensional. Determined
maximum temperature of the cable conductor is compared with
the optimal temperature for cable operation, given by the cable
producer, (i.e. 65 C). The computations considered the current
loading and volumetric heat generation rate from the cable
conductor
as
temperature-dependent.
The
cable
conductor
maximum temperature determined from FEM model shall not
exceed the optimum temperature of cable operation. Therefore,
PSO algorithm employs the dynamic penalty function which
eliminates the particles at a higher temperature than the optimal
one from the swarm.
Although the patterns of design variables variation with
increasing iteration number are different the obtained ﬁnal design
variables values are practically the same for Npar ¼ 10 and Npar ¼ 30:
 cable spacing l equal to 0.600 m;
 height and width of the FTB bedding cross-section are equal to
0.5133 m and 1.6 m, respectively;
 maximum cable core temperature, according to producer
recommendation, Topt ¼ 65 C.
The physical geometry of the modeled underground power ca-
ble system and the expected pattern of the thermal solution reveal
the mirror symmetry. Therefore, Ab ¼ 0.3195 m2 obtained from the
optimization is, in fact, only a half of the real FTB cross-sectional
area, which is equal to 0.6390 m2. It should be noted that the
exact value of width for FTB bedding layer is two times larger than
obtained from the optimization since the symmetry boundary
condition is applied in the FEM model.
